# Isolated scalar gravity: verified field solver and dimensional source transfer

## Outcome

The bounded scalar candidates now have an isolated, nonperiodic field solver for
named analytic axisymmetric sources. The implementation resolves their
nonspherical QUMOND response rather than applying an algebraic multiplier to
each source separately. It retains all Newtonian component fields before the
nonlinear step and uses the isolated Poisson Green function for the anomaly.

Eighteen joint candidate solves transfer the same a0=5e-11 m/s^2 and each fixed
shape m to an illustrative disk and spherical cluster. This is the lower a0
scenario retained by the [external-field audit](GRAVITY_EXTERNAL_QUADRUPOLE_2026_09_05.md).
It is not a galaxy-fitted or confirmed universal constant. The six earlier
Cassini-summary comparisons at this a0 remain development screens, not a full
Solar System pass.

The largest difference from the local algebraic shortcut in the dimensional
disk experiment is **3.944% in force**, exceeding the observed numerical
refinement changes. A field solve matters for the next observational test.
No galaxy or cluster observation was accessed or fitted in this experiment.

## Sources, equation, and boundary conditions

Each named stellar or gas component has a smooth nonnegative
[Miyamoto-Nagai](https://academic.oup.com/pasj/article/27/4/533/8122399) density,
with Newtonian potential

\[
\psi_i(R,z)=-\frac{G M_i}{\sqrt{R^2+[a_i+\sqrt{(z-z_i)^2+b_i^2}]^2}},
\quad M_i>0,\ a_i\geq0,\ b_i>0.
\]

Setting a_i=0 gives a Plummer sphere. The solver does not accept a zero-thickness
sheet. Its analytic gradients, Hessians and density satisfy finite-difference
and Poisson checks. Source masses and lengths describe the material scene;
they are not additional gravity-law constants. The physical run converts solar
mass inputs to GM using the same heliocentric GM as the local audit, and uses
consistent kpc and km/s units. A dimensional-rescaling test checks the adapter.

For the total psi=sum_i psi_i and p=grad psi, the anomalous Poisson source is

\[
S_{\rm an}=\nabla\cdot[(\nu-1)\mathbf p]
=(\nu-1)\nabla^2\psi+
\frac{\nu'(|\mathbf p|/a_0)}{a_0|\mathbf p|}\,
\mathbf p^T H(\psi)\mathbf p.
\]

The implementation extends the derivative coefficient continuously at a
zero-gradient saddle using the finite epsilon response. The mass sources are
summed before computing this expression. Splitting an identical density into
two differently named components leaves the field unchanged; summing separately
modified fields does not. A finite-difference flux-divergence check validates
the source formula. The bounded response and its action have not been changed.

The [QUMOND field equation](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/403/2/886/1184577)
requires a potential generated by this source. The algebraic vector
nu*Newtonian-acceleration generally has curl, so it cannot replace that potential
solution in a nonspherical source. This is a known distinction, not a new
gravity mechanism discovered here.

Expand S_an in Legendre modes S_l(r). The isolated Green-function solution is

\[
\phi_l(r)=-\frac{1}{2l+1}\left[
r^{-l-1}\int_0^r S_l(s)s^{l+2}ds+
r^l\int_r^\infty S_l(s)s^{1-l}ds\right].
\]

Actual integrations use a declared finite radial shell. Interior and exterior
omitted source contributions are tested by moving both boundaries. There are no
periodic images, homogeneous background subtraction, or zero-flux enclosing
wall. The spherical exterior contribution changes only the interior potential
zero, but nonspherical tails affect forces and cannot be silently discarded.
Absolute potential values are not used as a redshift or time-delay prediction.

Radial integration uses exact exponentially weighted moments of a cubic source
interpolant in log radius. This avoids unstable huge powers of radius at high
multipole order. A sinh angular map resolves a fixed thickness near the disk
plane; its scale is the smallest input component thickness, not a fitted gravity
parameter. Forces are derivatives of one interpolated scalar potential. A curl
control checks that evaluation path.

## Numerical evidence and its limits

The dimensional disk uses grids (radial nodes, angular nodes, maximum l) of
(2049,256,64) and (4097,512,128). A third solve moves the inner boundary inward
by ten and the outer boundary outward by ten. The spherical scene needs only
l=0; it uses the same radial refinements and an eight-node angular control.
All three kernel shapes use all three grids in both scenes.

| Control | Measured result |
|---|---:|
| Maximum sampled vector-force change on refinement | 0.1507% |
| Maximum sampled change after moving radial boundaries | 0.00100% |
| Finest Newtonian disk reconstruction vs analytic force | 0.0849% |
| Spherical candidate field vs exact joint QUMOND relation | 3.19e-8 relative |
| Independent finite-volume anomaly force difference, 96x96 | 0.7315% RMS |
| Independent finite-volume anomaly force difference, 192x192 | 0.1160% RMS |

The previous cylindrical finite-volume code in `work/gravitylab/axisym.py` was
read and reused without alteration. It solves the same analytic anomalous source
on a different mesh and with a different operator. Its boundary potentials are
supplied by the multipole solution, so agreement tests the interior
discretization, not an independent boundary condition or source reconstruction.
The two finite-volume residuals are below 1e-10. Potential gauges are matched.

Fifteen new controls bring the focused suite to **114 passing tests**. These
include manufactured l=0,2,3 potentials with both angular samplings, the three
spherical candidate shapes, Newtonian disk reconstruction, component partition
invariance, flux divergence, curl, unit scaling and unsupported-input rejection.
All focused lint checks pass. Remote CI and the full repository suite are not
claimed. The historical work already contained isolated finite-volume controls;
this adds an adapter for the current action and a different numerical method.

These are empirical numerical checks at declared probes. They are not certified
global error bounds, general accuracy for arbitrary thin disks, or tests of
formation and dynamical stability. In the spherical cluster, moving the inner
boundary exposed a small error that radial refinement alone did not remove.
Both kinds of check remain necessary.

## Illustrative transfer with the same constants

The disk contains 5e10 solar masses of stars with (a,b)=(3,0.3) kpc and 1e10
solar masses of gas with (a,b)=(7,0.6) kpc. The spherical cluster contains 1e14
solar masses of gas with b=200 kpc and 5e11 solar masses in a central stellar
component with b=10 kpc. These are invented smooth sources. In particular, the
cluster has no explicit member-galaxy population and supplies no member-relative
dispersion or lensing prediction.

| Shape m | Disk circular speed at 12 kpc | Disk circular speed at 40 kpc | Cluster force/Newtonian at 300 kpc | Cluster force/Newtonian at 1000 kpc |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1/2 | 145.84 km/s | 147.54 km/s | 1.057 | 2.083 |
| 1 | 152.58 km/s | 160.32 km/s | 1.060 | 2.729 |
| 2 | 156.98 km/s | 161.44 km/s | 1.026 | 2.932 |

All sampled midplane radial forces are inward. The quoted circular speed is
sqrt(-R*a_R), a kinematic field diagnostic; it is not a measured rotation speed,
proof of stable orbits, or an equilibrium cluster velocity dispersion. Source
geometry, masses and the common a0 were unchanged between kernels. At 12 kpc
the disk's algebraic shortcut differs by 1.26%, 2.39% and 3.94% for m=1/2,1,2.
These are method differences on invented sources, not observational residuals.
