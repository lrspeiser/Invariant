# Gravity hidden-variable and theory search goals

## Stable goal pointer

Use this exact repository-relative path when a goal, task, or automation needs to refer
to this roadmap:

`docs/GRAVITY_HIDDEN_VARIABLE_AND_THEORY_SEARCH_GOALS.md`

Suggested goal language:

> Use `docs/GRAVITY_HIDDEN_VARIABLE_AND_THEORY_SEARCH_GOALS.md` as the canonical

Referencing this document defines the search space and its tests. It does **not** by
itself authorize paid API calls, opening sealed confirmation data, weakening a frozen
gate, or bypassing the ordered program in `NO_DARK_MATTER_GRAVITY_DISCOVERY_GOALS.md`.

## Central target

Search for a universal, measurable cause `Z` and a derived function `F(Z)` such that the
effective coefficient observed in the current cross-scale diagnostic can be generated as

`beta_eff = (1/2) F(Z)`, with `F(Z) approximately 1` for disk galaxies and
`F(Z) approximately 4` for clusters.

This is a diagnostic target, not an established law. The ultimate theory must predict
direct observables from baryons and universal gravitational fields, without galaxy or
cluster identity, a fitted dark halo, a target-derived gravity/lensing mass, or a separate
gravitational constant for each object.

## Search boundary

- Allowed inputs: measured baryonic distributions, geometry, gradients, thermodynamic
  state, time/history proxies, environment, and universal fields or constants.
- Forbidden shortcuts: object identifiers, observed rotation speed as an input to its own
  prediction, GR/NFW-derived target mass, lensing-derived gravity supplied as a feature,
  or per-object gravitational constants hidden inside a fit.
- Creativity rule: keep speculative candidates alive long enough to test them. Label each
  proposal as a known formula, an algebraic rewrite, a known-family combination, or a
  potentially new synthesis; never use that label as a substitute for derivation or data.
- Failure rule: retain counterexamples and characterize excluded equation families so
  later searches avoid retrying the same failed region while preserving genuinely
  different ideas.
- Advancement rule: each hypothesis family must face its stated real, held-out problem
  before the roadmap advances on the strength of that family.

## A. Hidden-variable and first-principles theory search

1. **Effective dimension** — derive whether disks, spheres, filaments, or a continuous dimensionality variable changes the gravitational response; test galaxies versus clusters.
2. **Shape and anisotropy** — use flattening, triaxiality, orientation, and quadrupole structure to predict `F(Z)` without an object label.
3. **Surface versus volume density** — test whether a dimensionless combination of surface and volume density explains the cross-scale coefficient.
4. **Baryonic compactness** — derive and test dependence on mass-to-size or potential-depth ratios.
5. **Pressure support** — include rotational, thermal, turbulent, and velocity-dispersion support in one observable dynamics relation.
6. **Thermodynamic state** — test temperature, entropy, cooling time, and pressure gradients as causal variables rather than identifiers.
7. **Baryonic composition** — test universal dependence on gas, stars, plasma, molecular matter, and their spatial mixtures.
8. **Field gradients and curvature** — generate laws from acceleration gradients, baryonic-potential curvature, tidal invariants, and higher derivatives.
9. **Interior/exterior balance** — test whether the distribution of baryons inside and outside a radius predicts a redirected or nonlocal response.
10. **Baryonic boundaries** — derive surface, edge, shell, interface, and finite-domain terms that can focus gravity back toward baryonic regions.
11. **External baryonic field** — test neighboring baryons, large-scale structure, and tidal environment under strict target blindness.
12. **Dynamical age** — test formation-time and settling-time proxies for a universal history-dependent response.
13. **Relaxation and mergers** — test equilibrium, disturbance, and merger-state observables as predictors of deviations.
14. **Resonance and coherence** — derive whether long-lived orbital, field, or collective modes synchronize outer and inner speeds over cosmic time.
15. **Timescale ratios** — generate dimensionless laws from orbital, crossing, cooling, free-fall, relaxation, and cosmic timescale ratios.
16. **QED-like gravity** — construct a quantum-field-style grammar with carriers, vertices, propagators, loop corrections, running couplings, and polarization while enforcing a classical limit.
17. **Running gravitational strength** — allow the effective coupling to grow or shrink with distance, momentum, curvature, density, or environment; test one universal running law.
18. **Gravitational antiscreening** — test whether vacuum or field self-interactions strengthen gravity at large scales while satisfying local constraints.
19. **Massive gravitational particles** — explore Yukawa-like carriers and other finite-range modes with universal masses and couplings.
20. **Massive phases or periods** — allow gravity to enter long-duration or scale-dependent phases in which a massive mode dominates, then derive the transitions rather than fitting them per object.
21. **Mixed massive and massless modes** — test universal interference or crossover between long-range and finite-range gravitational degrees of freedom.
22. **Multiple gravitational polarizations** — explore scalar, vector, and tensor responses and require the same fields to predict motion and lensing.
23. **Bimetric or multi-metric gravity** — generate interacting-metric candidates with explicit matter coupling, stability, and observable limits.
24. **Different behavior of time** — allow clock rate, lapse, temporal dimension, or time response to depend on environment and derive both dynamics and light propagation.
25. **Time-varying gravity** — test universal cosmic or local evolution of gravitational strength against present-day and historical constraints.
26. **Retarded gravity** — explore finite-propagation and baryonic-history kernels instead of instantaneous response.
27. **Gravitational memory** — test whether the field retains a causal, fading record of earlier baryonic configurations.
28. **Periodic gravity** — generate spatial, temporal, logarithmic-scale, or phase oscillations and test their common wavelength and phase rules.
29. **Nonlinear self-interaction** — derive scale changes from the gravitational field's own energy or nonlinear equations rather than object-specific parameters.
30. **Screening mechanisms** — explore chameleon, symmetron, Vainshtein-like, and newly generated screening structures with galaxy-to-local-gravity continuity.
31. **Vacuum polarization or permittivity** — model the vacuum as a gravitational medium whose response is derived from local invariants and universal constants.
32. **Boundary focusing** — formalize the idea that gravity bends or redirects into baryon-rich regions and test the resulting vector/tensor field, not merely radial curve fits.
33. **Phase-transition gravity** — derive universal critical variables and crossovers between weak-field regimes.
34. **Condensate or superfluid behavior** — explore coherent gravitational-field phases while distinguishing new gravity from relabeled unseen matter.
35. **Modified inertia** — generate action-level candidates in which response to force changes, then test noncircular motion and conservation laws.
36. **Extra dimensions** — search compactified, warped, emergent, or scale-dependent dimensional models with observable four-dimensional limits.
37. **Alternative geometry** — explore torsion, nonmetricity, Finsler-like, affine, or other geometric invariants beyond metric curvature.
38. **Emergent gravity** — derive the effective law from thermodynamic, entropic, information-theoretic, or collective microscopic variables.
39. **Holographic or boundary gravity** — test whether bulk behavior follows from baryonic or spacetime boundary data under a universal mapping.
40. **Discrete or network gravity** — generate graph, lattice, causal-set, and connectivity-based laws whose continuum limits are explicit.
41. **Stochastic gravity** — test whether universal field fluctuations or noise-induced drift create reproducible scale-dependent means and variances.
42. **Matter-geometry feedback** — derive nonlinear feedback in which baryonic organization changes geometry and geometry changes baryonic motion.
43. **Cosmological boundary coupling** — test universal dependence on expansion, horizon, background curvature, or large-scale boundary conditions.
44. **Scale hierarchy** — generate laws from ratios among baryonic size, gravitational transition length, carrier wavelength, curvature radius, and horizon scale.
45. **Universal interaction variables** — automatically synthesize dimensionless cross-terms among geometry, density, gradients, time, environment, and field invariants.

## B. Discovery engine

46. **Dimensionless generator** — enumerate physically admissible dimensionless variables with Buckingham-Pi-style reduction before fitting formulas.
47. **Operator generator** — generate local, nonlocal, differential, integral, tensor, and history-dependent operators with dimensional and symmetry constraints.
48. **Action generator** — construct candidate Lagrangians/actions first, derive field equations automatically, and reject candidates that fail basic identities.
49. **Pseudorandom exploration** — sample reproducible, seeded equation programs across the full grammar and record exactly how many candidates and equivalence classes were tested.
50. **LLM creativity** — use model ensembles to propose mechanisms and cross-domain syntheses, with explicit known/rewrite/combination/new-synthesis labels and independent verification.
51. **GPU screening** — use the RTX 5090 for vectorized numerical rejection of huge candidate batches, after measuring the actual evaluator throughput rather than claiming unsupported trillions.
52. **Failure-space database** — store counterexamples, failure causes, and certified regions of equation space that cannot pass the frozen constraints.
53. **Diversity preservation** — maintain novelty archives and multiple mechanism niches so early fit scores do not prune structurally different ideas too quickly.
54. **Equivalence detection** — merge algebraic rewrites, rescalings, renamed parameters, and behaviorally identical programs without erasing genuinely distinct mechanisms.
55. **Causal-variable tests** — use ablations, matched subsets, interventions where possible, and out-of-distribution splits to distinguish causes from dataset labels or proxies.

## C. Empirical and formal gates

56. **Disk-galaxy gate** — predict held-out radial rotation-curve measurements across the admitted SPARC exploration galaxies from baryons and universal fields.
57. **Independent-galaxy gate** — test the unchanged survivor on independent THINGS and LITTLE THINGS pipelines and then on the still-sealed SPARC confirmation set when authorized.
58. **Cluster-coefficient gate** — predict the cluster-scale `beta_eff` shift from directly measured baryonic geometry or state, not cluster identity or inferred total mass.
59. **X-COP gate** — forward-predict X-ray and SZ observables for held-out radii and held-out clusters with declared pressure and member-baryon nuisances.
60. **Direct-CLASH lensing gate** — predict image positions, parities, shapes, shear, magnification, and time delays from calibrated light and gas data, never a GR/NFW-derived mass profile as the target.
61. **Cross-scale gate** — require one law and one universal parameter set to cover galaxies, groups, clusters, and their transition regime.
62. **Time-variation gate** — confront any evolving-gravity candidate with clocks, orbital histories, stellar evolution, cosmology, and bounds on changing constants.
63. **Massive-mode gate** — confront massive-carrier candidates with Solar-System, binary-pulsar, gravitational-wave dispersion, polarization, and range constraints.
64. **Distance-running gate** — test one preregistered running-coupling curve across laboratory, Solar-System, galaxy, cluster, and cosmological scales.
65. **Lensing-slip gate** — require the same fields to predict dynamical and lensing potentials, with any gravitational slip derived rather than fitted separately.
66. **Conservation gate** — verify stress-energy conservation, constraint propagation, gauge identities, and a coherent matter-coupling prescription.
67. **Stability gate** — reject ghosts, gradient instabilities, tachyonic regimes, unbounded Hamiltonians, or catastrophically short instability times.
68. **Causality gate** — verify hyperbolicity, well-posed evolution, characteristic cones, and acceptable propagation behavior.
69. **Strong-field gate** — test Cassini, planetary dynamics, binary pulsars, black-hole observations, and gravitational waves without retuning the weak-field law.
70. **Cosmology gate** — test expansion, nucleosynthesis, CMB, BAO, structure growth, and cosmological lensing without silently reintroducing dark matter under another name.
71. **Independent confirmation** — freeze code, data transformations, exclusions, priors, and predictions before opening new objects or commissioning an independent replay.
72. **Novelty adjudication** — search prior art, prove non-equivalence where possible, distinguish known formulas from genuine synthesis, and limit every claim to what independent data and derivations establish.

## Required receipt for every attempted item

Each attempt must leave a replayable record containing: item number; hypothesis; provenance
and creativity label; mathematical definition; dimensional and symmetry checks; data-source
receipt; frozen exploration/confirmation boundary; baselines; compute and API cost; candidate
and equivalence-class counts; counterexamples; result (`PASS`, `REJECT`, `INCONCLUSIVE`, or
`BLOCKED`); and the exact next action justified by that result.
