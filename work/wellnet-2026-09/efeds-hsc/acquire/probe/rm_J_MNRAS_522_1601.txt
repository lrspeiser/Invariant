Abstract:
    We present the first cosmological study of a sample of eROSITA
    clusters, which were identified in the eROSITA Final Equatorial Depth
    Survey (eFEDS). In a joint selection on X-ray and optical observables,
    the sample contains 455 clusters within a redshift range of 0.1 < z < 1.2,
    of which 177 systems are covered by the public data from the Hyper
    Suprime-Cam (HSC) survey that enables uniform weak-lensing cluster
    mass constraints. With minimal assumptions, at each cluster redshift
    we empirically model (1) the scaling relations between the cluster
    halo mass and the observables, which include the X-ray count rate, the
    optical richness, and the weak-lensing mass, and (2) the X-ray
    selection in terms of the completeness function C. Using the richness
    distribution of the clusters, we directly measure the X-ray
    completeness and adopt those measurements as informative priors for
    the parameters of C. In a blinded analysis, we obtain the cosmological
    constraints {omega}_m_=0.245^+0.048^_-0.058_,
    {sigma}_8_=0.833^+0.075^_-0.063_, and
    S_8_ = {sigma}_8_({omega}_m_/0.3)^0.3^ = 0.791^+0.028^_-0.031_ in a
    flat LCDM cosmology. Extending to a flat wCDM cosmology leads to the
    constraint on the equation of state parameter of the dark energy of
    w = -1.25 +/- 0.47. The eFEDS constraints are in good agreement with
    the results from the Planck mission, the galaxy-galaxy lensing and
    clustering analysis of the Dark Energy Survey, and the cluster
    abundance analysis of the SPT-SZ survey at a level of <~ 1{sigma}.
    With the empirical modelling, this work presents the first fully
    self-consistent cosmological constraints based on a synergy between
    wide-field X-ray and weak lensing surveys.

Description:
    In this paper, we continue to constrain cosmology by combining the
    weak-lensing mass calibration presented in Chiu et al.
    (2022A&A...661A..11C, Cat. J/A+A/661/A11) with the abundance of the
    eFEDS clusters. We stress that the eFEDS clusters used in this work
    are first selected in X-rays and then confirmed in the optical,
    resulting in a clean and sizable sample for cosmological study without
    losing a significant amount of clusters due to a more stringent X-ray
    selection. We use the sample of clusters in the eROSITA Final
    Equatorial Depth Survey (eFEDS), of which the data and catalogues are
    publicly available within the eROSITA Early Data Release. The data
    were processed by the pipeline eSASSusers_201009, which is described
    in detail in Brunner et al. (2022A&A...661A...1B, Cat. J/A+A/661/A1).
    After the source detection, the catalogue of eFEDS clusters is
    constructed using the criteria of Ldet > 5, Lext > 6, and EXT > 0,
    resulting in the 542 clusters that are publicly released in Liu et al.
    (2022A&A...661A...2L, Cat. J/A+A/661/A2).

    Along the section 5, we first present the results obtained from
    independently modelling the mass calibration and the cluster
    abundance, and then the results of the joint analysis. Finally, the
    cosmological constraints are presented. We focus on the modelling
    assuming a flat LCDM cosmology and then extend the results to the flat
    wCDM cosmology. Then, with the mass posterior P(M|{eta},z,p) for each
    cluster we randomly sample a mass estimate from the posterior as the
    'ensemble' mass. The ensemble mass here is referred to as the
    individual mass estimate that acts as an ensemble statistic inferred
    from the overall population modelling. The uncertainty of the cluster
    mass, which is derived as the dispersion of the mass posterior, is at
    a level of about 20 per cent for a cluster with 
    M_500_c =~ 10^14^ h^-1^ M_{sun}_. We provide these mass measurements
    in tableb1.dat of the same 542 sources.
