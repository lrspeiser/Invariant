G4  Flat rotation curves: theory first, then numbers
==============================================================================
   THEOREM 1.  For a point source Phi = -G M F(r)/r exactly, so
      v_c^2 = G M (F/r - F') = (G M F / r) (1 - dlnF/dlnr).
      Every family here has qbar in [0,1) and Tbar finite, so F is
      bounded: sup F = 1+alpha (F1), e^alpha (F2), 1+alpha/(1+beta) (F3).
      Hence r v_c^2 -> G M F_inf: the curve is exactly Keplerian at
      large r, with G renormalised by F_inf.  Asymptotically flat is
      impossible for the whole family.  Numerically:
      F1_poly   alpha=3.0  sup F= 4.000   r v^2/GM at 30 Mpc = 3.9998 (1.0000 of sup),  dlnv/dlnr = -0.5000  (Kepler = -0.5)
      F2_exp    alpha=1.0  sup F= 2.718   r v^2/GM at 30 Mpc = 2.7181 (0.9999 of sup),  dlnv/dlnr = -0.5000  (Kepler = -0.5)
      F3_pade   alpha=5.0  sup F= 3.500   r v^2/GM at 30 Mpc = 3.4999 (1.0000 of sup),  dlnv/dlnr = -0.5000  (Kepler = -0.5)
   
   THE NAIVE ARGUMENT IS WRONG.  'q is roughly constant on galaxy
      scales, so F is roughly constant, so Phi is a rescaled Newtonian
      potential and v^2 ~ 1/r' drops the -G M F' term.  That term is
      not small: v^2 = (G M F/r)(1 - dlnF/dlnr), so an F rising with
      logarithmic slope approaching 1 flattens the curve completely.
      Size of the dropped term, MW-like model, smooth q, alpha = 3:
      r/kpc       3.0    5.5    9.9   18.1   33.0   60.0
      F_eff     0.421  0.664  0.927  1.281  2.172  2.988
      dlnF/dlnr 0.819  0.662  0.478  0.752  0.760  0.398
      max dlnF/dlnr = 0.899; the naive argument assumes 0.
   
   THEOREM 2.  v_c^2 < 0 whenever dlnF/dlnr > 1.  Flat rotation sits on
      the boundary of a REPULSIVE regime: to flatten a curve F must
      rise with logarithmic slope tending to 1, and any overshoot
      removes circular orbits entirely.  A q that switches sharply --
      which the clipped delta form does by construction, since
      q = rho_ref/rho_s - 1 is a near-step function of density -- always
      overshoots.
   
   THEOREM 3.  The only F making v_c exactly flat is F = C r ln(r_*/r),
      C = v_f^2/(G M).  Two bounds on the radial stretch it can cover:
      (a) unconstrained, apex of F on the ceiling  : r2/r1 from the two
          roots of C r ln(r_*/r) = 1;
      (b) with F(r1) = 1 and F'(r1) = 0, which is what an inner
          rotation curve fitted by baryons alone demands, then
          v_f^2 = G M F(r1)/r1 <= G M F(r2)/r2 gives r2/r1 <= sup F.
      alpha = 0.3   sup F = 1.30   r2/r1 (unconstrained) =     4.35   r2/r1 (F'(r1)=0) <=   1.30   |v^2 - const| = 8.9e-16
      alpha = 1.0   sup F = 2.00   r2/r1 (unconstrained) =    11.55   r2/r1 (F'(r1)=0) <=   2.00   |v^2 - const| = 8.9e-16
      alpha = 3.0   sup F = 4.00   r2/r1 (unconstrained) =    36.26   r2/r1 (F'(r1)=0) <=   4.00   |v^2 - const| = 8.9e-16
      alpha = 10.0  sup F = 11.00  r2/r1 (unconstrained) =   144.68   r2/r1 (F'(r1)=0) <=  11.00   |v^2 - const| = 8.9e-16
      alpha = 30.0  sup F = 31.00  r2/r1 (unconstrained) =   522.02   r2/r1 (F'(r1)=0) <=  31.00   |v^2 - const| = 8.9e-16
      A decade of exactly flat curve therefore needs alpha >= 9 under
      the physical constraint, or alpha ~ 1 without it.

G6  Cost of the double integral, and the price of accelerating it
==============================================================================
   Cost model.  N_f field points, N_s source cells, n_s path samples:
      direct  O(N_f N_s n_s) trilinear interpolations
      grid of n^3 cells evaluated at every cell -> O(n^6 n_s)
      n = 64 : 6.9e10 pair-samples;  n = 128 : 4.4e12.
   The spherical and axisymmetric reductions used for the rotation-curve
   work collapse this to O(N_f N_r' N_D n_s) ~ 1e7, which is why the

G7  What only a nonlocal kernel predicts, and how big it is
==============================================================================
   The signature is that two sources at the SAME separation couple
   differently when the material BETWEEN them differs.  No point-local
   law -- MOND, the RAR, f(R), any mu(g) or mu(rho) -- can produce it,
   because those depend only on fields evaluated at one point.
   
   But the signature and the galaxy application both draw on the SAME
   single global density scale rho_ref, and they want opposite values.
   This gate measures that tension rather than asserting it.
