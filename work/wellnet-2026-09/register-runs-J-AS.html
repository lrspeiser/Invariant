Tier 1 · live

Two results, and neither is the one I expected

The programme set out to find a new force law. It has not found one. What it has found is a clean parameter-free success for a law that already existed, and a well-audited anomaly that no law on the table explains — including the ones this programme was promoting.

Live 01 · Run AL

The RAR lands on raw cluster shear with no new gravity parameter, near no slip

496 eFEDS systems, 3,365 raw DECADE tangential-shear points, gravitational slip fixed at Σs = 1 exactly, a0 frozen on SPARC galaxy rotation curves. No new gravity parameter is fitted — though the pipeline still carries X-ray profile fits, gas conversions, stellar masses, centring, source redshifts, shear calibration and distance factors, so this is a parameter-free law, not a parameter-free measurement. The column on the right is the lensing response each law would need to match the data — 1.00 means the photons couple exactly as general relativity says they should.

The RAR and AQUAL need a lensing response of 0.98 and 0.99. That is a central value 1.9% from no slip — not slip measured to 2%. The uncertainty is a factor of two, dominated by unpublished X-ray profile covariance, and the null-corrected bracket runs 1.06–2.17. It is still the cleanest positive result the cluster work has produced.

The two rows are also one result reported twice: in spherical symmetry AQUAL reduces to an algebraic μ-relation that approximates the RAR by construction, so χ² = 3588.4 against 3588.2 is not two independent confirmations. And the RAR does not itself specify how light propagates. The honest object is RAR/AQUAL-like dynamics + spherical projection + a no-slip closure.

It also kills the programme's own best candidate in passing: both tidal variants fit worse than predicting no lensing signal at all, and both require η < 0 — light bending the wrong way relative to matter.

Why it is not decided

Strong lensing disagrees with weak. Against SN Refsdal's 1.48% time delay, Newton is the only law of the five for which a single universal slip serves both regimes — its bracket contains 1. Every MOND-like law needs 2 to 6 times more lensing response in the cluster core than in the group outskirts. But that is an effective closure mismatch, not yet evidence for regime-dependent photon coupling: it is equally consistent with core-baryon modelling, line-of-sight structure, triaxial deprojection, mass-sheet degeneracy, or the assumed closure. And a single time delay can never test a gravity law — one closure parameter brings every law, Newton included, onto Refsdal’s 376.02 d.

What would settle it

The dominant uncertainty is not the shear — it is that Bahar+2022 publishes no covariance for its X-ray density parameters, so Σs carries a factor-two bracket set by a null that drags it down by up to 2.2× at 17σ. Publishing the Vikhlinin covariance would take this measurement from a factor of two to about 10%. That is a request to an author, not a new observing programme.

Live 02 · Run AF

Something in the vertical direction is real, and its cause is unresolved

The between-galaxy trend in vertical support across a factor of 35 in central surface density. This is the first result in the programme to survive a complete shared-denominator audit — the audit that has now killed six other correlations.

Pushing the complete observational covariance through the real pipeline at a true Newtonian Bz = 1, the recovered slope never reached −0.346 in 2,000 trials (worst case −0.325, p ≤ 5.0×10−4). Producing it as an artefact needs a photometric error of 0.70 mag against the tabulated 0.070. The closed-form estimator, which never touches the forward chain, gives an implied −0.332 — the signal is in the raw catalogue numbers.

The slope itself does not reject the RAR or AQUAL — at 0.32–0.82σ and 0.47–1.22σ it agrees with them. What fails is the joint amplitude-and-shape prediction (ΔAIC +17.9 over Newton, +8.8 over the RAR, calibrated against their own truth-nulls), driven by the within-galaxy radial profile rather than the between-galaxy trend.

Why this is not evidence for a modified force law

The winning model and “Newton with ΥK ∝ Σ0p” give identical amplitudes and identical scale lengths to 5×10−15 dex — machine precision, because every term is exactly linear in Σ*0. A stellar mass-to-light ratio that varies with surface density reproduces it exactly. That degeneracy can only be broken from outside DiskMass.

The one piece of evidence against the systematic reading

The required ΥK trend is −0.395 dex/dex — and it is opposite in sign to the observed B−K colour trend. The alternative, hz/hR varying by a factor 5.2, sits against Bershady's measured 1.22.

Tier 2 · capability

Most of a search can be decided before the data is opened

This is the result the programme is most likely to be remembered for, and it is a methodological one. An admissibility compiler applies five invariance requirements — potential gauge, constant-tensor coordinate degeneracy, mass partition and coarse-graining, reciprocity, and variational consistency — to a candidate law without evaluating it against anything.

It was validated first: 35 of 35 tests pass, with no disagreement against any verdict the programme had already recorded by other means, reproducing them to three or more decimals. Then it was run backwards over the completed tournament. All 18 survivors fail a gate. Seven carry a mass exponent of exactly zero, which selective refinement forbids — meaning how finely a different galaxy is tabulated sets the field near an untouched one. That was decidable before a single member galaxy was drawn.

Cost: 31–47 seconds on one CPU core against the tournament's four data channels. The family-verdict lookup runs at 4–7 million settings per second, two to three times the throughput of the search it would front.

It was then confirmed from a direction that shares no code and no data with it. The orthogonal-orbit lane measured the curl of the field each surviving law actually produces — 3.8×10−5 for Newton (its estimator's noise floor), 0.048 for the RAR, 0.054 for the well-network tensor and 1.08 for the tidal scalar. This shows the algebraic prescription is not derivable from a single static scalar potential — not that it cannot come from an action, which was my error: the Lorentz force has curl and comes from an action. The identity ∇×g = (∇ν)×gN makes the RAR’s 0.048 expected rather than anomalous, and AQUAL exists precisely to supply the completion. What survives is the magnitude: the tidal scalar sits 22× the RAR.

A related pattern is now hard to ignore. This programme has proved four separate blindness theorems — spherical blindness, the QUMOND tensor degeneracy, boundedness, and now the fact that Adyn ≡ 1 for any law of the form F(scalar invariants) ∇ΦN. Each time, an observable the programme reached for turned out to be provably insensitive to the structure it was reaching for, and each time that was knowable in advance. The compiler exists because of this pattern, not in spite of it.

Two corrections that go with it

The 97.2% mixes four verdicts that are not equivalent: mathematically inconsistent (a real kill), incomplete as written (a variational completion may exist), convention-dependent (kills the formula, not every potential-dependent theory), and non-identifiable on this bench (an experiment cannot see it — the theory is not thereby inconsistent). Only the first is unconditional. And 35/35 against this programme’s own verdicts is regression testing, which risks validating the compiler against the conclusions that shaped it; external positive controls are running.

And the correction from last round

The previous version of this page claimed the machinery was “good enough to trust a positive.” Withdrawn. Against an arbitrary smooth scalar response — rather than an off-grid member of the bank's own grammar — the tensor detector's false-positive rate is 0.092, not the nominal 0.05 and not the 0.042 previously reported. The null is heavy-tailed: the two families genuinely outside the grammar produce apparent detections 150–200× their own median. Deprojection error alone contributes 0.15.

Tier 3 · retired

What died since this page last claimed it was alive

Kept rather than deleted, because a register that quietly drops its predictions is not a register.

Retired · was Lead 01

Potential depth predicting the cluster amplitude

Reported here as “a 63% reduction in out-of-sample cluster error from one parameter, fitted without ever seeing a cluster.”

How it failed

The 63% figure was blind to its own parameter — the first and largest of the corrections below. Tested properly against raw shear it found nothing. Given four separate environmental variables on one sample with one set of folds, the largest signal belongs to a bare acceleration tilt with no environmental content at all (z = +5.97, above potential depth's +4.93). And the compiler's gauge gate shows the depth response spans 2.07 dex across boundary rules — the on/off verdict itself changes depending on where you put the zero of the potential.

Retired · was Lead 02

The nonlocal path kernel

Reported here at “0.156 dex against Newton's 0.646.”

How it failed

That comparison was apples-to-oranges; measured on the same footing it is 0.256/0.209 against the RAR's 0.121/0.122. The structural obstruction turned out to be sharper than the one I named: it is not that F is bounded but that every available invariant decays outward, so no member of the family can produce a flat curve at any amplitude. Numerics sound, physics dead.

Retired · the tournament's best survivor

The scalar gated on the tidal invariant

The one candidate that reached the end of a four-channel tournament of 3,123 laws, and the only structure that separates a galaxy from a cluster shell by two orders of magnitude. Four independent lanes then took it apart.

How it failed, five ways

Its headline amplitude was contaminated: A = 16 was selected against a requirement interpolated from published lensing masses, which assume the very slip it was being scored on. It is not environmental: on a spherical source the tidal invariant reduces analytically to √6 (g/r)|1−ρ/⟨ρ⟩|, so its advantage over potential depth is an advantage of g/r over Φ — a local-kinematic gate wearing an environmental name. Its linear cosmological response is identically zero: |T| vanishes in a homogeneous background and the frozen exponent is even, so (1−W) ∝ δ² (measured slope 1.9999998) and at linear order the candidate is exactly AQUAL with a0 → 17 a0. And it forms structure backwards: an isotropic configuration has |T| = 0 and so receives the full boost while a pancake receives a fifth of it — sphere/pancake 4.90 at z = 999, against the observed cosmic web. Anti-Zel'dovich. And its field does not come from an action: the measured curl of the field it actually produces is 1.08 in units where Newton's numerical floor is 3.8×10−5 and the RAR is 0.048 — the worst in the set by a factor of eight.

Tier 4 · what would still decide something

Where the remaining leverage actually is

  • The Vikhlinin covariance an email, not a telescope The dominant uncertainty on the only clean cluster result is an unpublished parameter covariance. It is the highest return per unit effort anywhere in the programme.
  • Cluster cores, for the tidal gate needs |T| ∼ T0 On eFEDS the gate is saturated — median W = 0.9998, only 0.45% of points below 0.90 — so it degenerates to AQUAL with a rescaled a0, which a universal slip absorbs exactly. Separation is 0.80σ. The programme's sharpest falsifiable claim is not testable on weak lensing at all, and more groups would not help. It needs ~1014 M⊙ inside a few hundred kpc.
  • An external-axis basis element costs nothing Not one of the 3,123 candidates carries an external tidal axis: 1,560 network, 780 source, 783 isotropic, 0 external. The 2-D shear phase channel is built, calibrated and pointed at a hypothesis the grammar does not contain.
  • Deeper shear on the phase channel 111× the source count The exclusion currently sits at an ellipticity of 2.11 — above the geometric maximum of 1. No external-axis tensor of any amplitude is excluded, so the present null means nothing. Its positive control does not fire either.
  • A published vc(R) covariance not more streams 81% of the orthogonal-orbit null variance is the shared baryon fit, and the ratio does not cancel it because the response is nonlinear in |gN|. Doubling the stream sample raised the error, 0.041 to 0.068. Together with the missing Vikhlinin covariance above, the programme's error budget is now twice dominated by a baryon model someone else published rather than by the measurement being made.
  • Anything outside DiskMass, vertically breaks a 5e−15 degeneracy The vertical anomaly is exactly degenerate with a surface-density-dependent mass-to-light ratio. One independent ΥK measurement decides whether it is gravity.
  • Void path length × Planck the one surviving branch Supernova time dilation kills every energy-drain redshift mechanism at 90σ. A geometric path stretch predicts b = 1 identically and is invisible to that test — but capped at 0.28–0.44% by the CMB, which is the only cheap probe that reaches it.
The phenomenon

The thing being chased is still there

Worth restating, because it is easy to lose in nine pages of methodology. On 40 LoCuSS clusters measured with Subaru weak lensing — free of hydrostatic circularity — the gravity excess over baryons-plus-RAR is real and large: median 1.62, ranging 1.22–2.34, a near-constant multiplicative offset with d ln E / d ln M = +0.31 and no temperature dependence.

And the cross-probe picture is now consistent everywhere except one place. Two blind holdouts land on the radial-acceleration relation. The KiDS bridge closed the 100–500 kpc gap. Clusters are the lone outlier, and what survives of the anomaly is a cluster-only excess organised by r/R500 — not by mass, not by temperature, and not by any environmental variable this programme has been able to construct.
