Nothing at Complexity Three

A GPU search that scores 77 million candidate gravity laws per second against real rotation curves, each one with its optimal coefficients. It enumerated every three-term law that the grammar can express — all 1.14 billion of them — and none of them beats the radial acceleration relation out of sample. The interesting part is not that they failed. It is that a twin dataset with the physics deliberately removed failed by almost exactly as much.

J.1 — The premise

A billion candidates that never met an observation

The repository already contained a billion-candidate screen. Its own manifest records observational_data_opened = False and synthetic_analytic_controls_only = True: a billion laws were screened for flatness, BTFR slope and Newtonian limits on idealised analytic curves, and not one was ever scored against a measurement. The number was real. The contact with data was not.

This run closes that gap, and does so at a scale where the honest question stops being “can we find something that fits?” and becomes “how much fit does a search of this size produce from nothing?”

J.2 — Architecture

Why billions are meaningful here

A candidate law is a functional form, not a coefficient vector. For any fixed set of basis functions the optimal coefficients solve a linear system, so sampling coefficients at random is a slow, bad way to rediscover what linear algebra gives exactly. The expensive axis is which basis functions appear.

So the search is an atom bank plus subset selection:

1. An atom is one parameterised basis function — e.g. 1/(1 + x/s) at one particular s — evaluated on every point-draw of the bench. Ten families × eight variables × a 25-point log scale grid = 1,898 atoms. 2. The atom Gram G = A Aᵀ and the projections v = A y are computed ONCE, on the training points only, in float64. 3. A candidate law is a SUBSET of K atoms. Its optimal coefficients and its exact training RMS come from a K×K sub-matrix GATHERED out of G. The data is never touched again.

Scoring one candidate with its optimal coefficients therefore costs a K×K Cholesky and O(K²) of memory traffic — about 10² flops instead of about 10⁵. That is the whole trick, and it is what makes the throughput real rather than rhetorical.

Encoding bug that looked like a solver bug

Atoms are standardised to zero mean, so a genome that happened to contain no intercept could not reach the data’s mean at all. Those rows scored ≈0.47 dex — worse than predicting nothing — which made every k = 6 result look catastrophic. The intercept is now carried in every model and is not counted toward K.

J.3 — What the rank-2 theorem licenses

Two directions, not one

The theorem established earlier on this bench is that the eight-variable set (aN, Σb, ρb, r, Mb, θ, Φb, environment) has numerical rank 2. It does not say that only functions of aN are available. It says there are exactly two independent directions, and that aN and r span them.

Every atom is therefore tagged by which direction it uses, so the report can state what a winning law needed instead of asserting it:

[g] the RAR's own direction — can only re-fit the interpolating function [r] the second independent direction — the only new point-local information [gal] galaxy-level constants, fixed along a rotation curve [nl] nonlocal functionals of the source, not a point function of either

An earlier draft of this module tagged r as “escaping the rank-2 span”, which was wrong — r is in the variable set the theorem covers. The theorem constrains dimension, not which of the two dimensions you are allowed to use.

J.4 — The controls

Three twins with the physics removed

Every candidate is scored against four targets that differ only in v and y·y, so all four cost the same gather:

real measured log₁₀(g_obs / g_bar) null RAR + Gaussian noise matched to the real residual scatter perm RAR + the real residuals permuted across ALL points perm_g RAR + the real residuals permuted WITHIN each galaxy

perm_g is the sharp one. It keeps every galaxy’s mean offset — distance, inclination and mass-to-light errors are real, and are not new physics — and destroys only the radial dependence. Anything the search extracts from it is search capacity, not gravity.

Each target is scored against its own baseline. Comparing a control to the real target’s baseline is a baseline mismatch, not a control.

J.5 — Result 1

The evolutionary run

4,000,000 population × 300 generations × 4 targets = 4.8 billion evaluations per seed, K = 8, roughly four minutes of wall clock each. Propose, score with exact coefficients, penalise complexity, prune by truncation, breed by uniform crossover, mutate by global jumps, local walks along the parameter grid, and collapses. Run at three independent seeds — 14.4 billion evaluations in total.

Read the control column first. A target with the radial physics deliberately destroyed recovers +3.1 of the +5.1 points the real data gives. Roughly five eighths of the apparent training gain is manufactured by the search itself.

And the remainder does not survive. On held-out galaxies, with coefficients frozen at their trained values, the best laws are 4 % worse than the RAR — while the physics-free controls, put through the identical shortlist-then-blind procedure, come out slightly ahead. The three seeds span 0.27 points on train and 0.30 on blind, and not one of the sixty shortlisted laws beat the RAR out of sample.

A bug that produced a false positive

The first blind evaluation called the scorer with the blind Gram — which refits the coefficients on the blind set. It reported +2.17 %. The correct procedure — fit on train, freeze, evaluate — reports −3.73 % for the same law. A held-out set that the fit is allowed to touch is not held out.

The monotonic pattern is the finding. The more complexity the search is allowed, the better it fits the training set and the worse it does out of sample. This is now demonstrated by enumeration over every possibility, not inferred from a search that might simply have looked in the wrong place.

At k = 1 the real data gains less than the physics-free control does. The RAR’s own interpolating function, with a₀ refitted, is already essentially optimal in its own direction. There is nothing left to win there.

Two things fall out of the enumeration

The evolutionary loop is validated as an instrument

The exhaustive k = 3 winner is exactly the law the evolutionary search found at k = 3 — nu_std(gbar/2.15) + nu_simp(r/0.841) + expneg(r/1.36), 0.18792 dex. The loop located the true global optimum, so its results at K = 8, where enumeration is impossible, can be trusted as searches rather than accidents.

The control behaves exactly as designed

The perm_g winner at k = 3 is built from two galaxy-level Mb atoms — it is exploiting precisely the per-galaxy offsets the control was constructed to preserve. That is the behaviour it exists to expose.

Every reported RMS is verified against a direct recomputation from the raw data, bypassing the Gram algebra entirely: maximum absolute difference 4.5 × 10⁻⁸ dex.

J.7 — Result 3

Option 1 of the well–mirror model

Option 1 — a perpendicular push projected onto a curved well — was omitted from the earlier mirror sweep. It is the formulation that actually answers the push-versus-pull question, so it is worth doing properly.

g_mirror(r) = A_u(r) · h′(r) / (1 + h′(r)²) g(r) = (1 − η) g_N(r) + η · g_mirror(r)

Option 1a — the ansatz as written

The proposal closes the system by defining g_mirror = g_N + V_W²/(r + r_t), so the mirror restores the η gN removed from the direct well and adds an edge term. Substituting collapses it:

g(r) = (1−η) g_N + η g_N + η V_W²/(r+r_t) = g_N(r) + η V_W²/(r + r_t)

η now appears only in the product η VW². Refitting at fixed η = 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 0.90 with everything else free gives train RMS 0.224752 at every value — spread 0.00 × 10⁰ dex, bit-identical. η is not a measurable push/pull ratio here; it is a relabelling of the edge-term amplitude, the same degeneracy already found in Options 2 and 3.

The form also fits worse than the RAR, and the structural reason is legible in the formula: as r → 0 the edge term tends to VW²/(λ rM), a constant rather than zero, so the model never reduces to Newton in the inner region.

Option 1b — the literal point mirror, where η is real

An attractive source at u = −d with an equal repulsive image at u = +d gives, on the middle surface, A_u(r) = 2GM_b d/(r²+d²)^{3/2}. Without inserting gN into the mirror by hand, η sets the coefficient of the direct Newtonian term independently of the mirror amplitude — and at small r the data pin it. So this version can be asked the question and made to answer.

The push fraction is driven to essentially zero, and η = ½ — the “half pull, half push” concept — is excluded by 0.12 dex of RMS. At its best η the model sits at 0.5327 train / 0.5427 blind against the RAR’s 0.1970 / 0.1768: as η → 0 it degenerates to pure Newtonian gravity, which is what 0.53 dex is.

This is the outcome the proposal’s own text anticipated — a point-like mirror gives Au ∼ 1/r³ at large r and cannot sustain the 1/r field a flat rotation curve needs. The data agree quantitatively, in the one formulation where the push/pull ratio is a measurable quantity rather than a reparameterisation.

J.8 — Register

What was established

Nothing was discovered. What was established is stronger than another falsification of one formula.

  1. A proof at low complexity Over this atom bank, no law of complexity ≤ 3 beats the RAR out of sample, and none beats the physics-free controls by more than the controls beat themselves. This is enumeration, not sampling.
  2. A calibrated overfitting scale for this bench A physics-free twin with only per-galaxy offsets preserved yields +3.2 % of training gain at K = 8 and +2.1 % at k = 3. Any future claim on SPARC of a few per cent improvement over the RAR now has a number to clear.
  3. A validated instrument The evolutionary loop recovered the exhaustive global optimum at k = 3, so its K = 8 results — where enumeration is impossible — can be trusted.
  4. The push/pull ratio is settled Unidentifiable in Options 1a, 2 and 3 by construction; in Option 1b, where it is identifiable, the data set it to ≈ 0.05 and exclude 0.5.

The honest reading

This bench is exhausted for point-local laws. There are two independent directions; one is already used optimally by the RAR, and the second buys training fit that does not generalise. Progress requires a probe that adds a direction — the vertical / anisotropy channel, or cluster scales where the RAR is known to fail — not more search in this space. The search machinery is now fast enough that the binding constraint is unambiguously the data, not the compute.

Holdouts

KiDS and wide binaries remain sealed and are not present in any module described here. The blind split was touched exactly once per run, by the final shortlist, with coefficients frozen on train.
