# Independent analytic review

The proposed static equations are consistent. Completing the oscillator square gives omega²(q-h)²/2 minus omega²h²/2, so minimizing in q yields U_extra=-C*m_i*m_j*ln(1+L/s), C=omega²*eta²/2 and s=sqrt(r²+b²). The inward acceleration magnitude per test mass is C*M*L*r/[s²(s+L)]. The sign is attractive; C has units length²/(mass*time²), making both energy and acceleration dimensionally correct.

Differentiating M_effective(r)=r²*g/G gives exactly the positive density stated in the protocol. Direct independent mass quadrature and a five-point potential derivative verify those identities. The density is finite at the center for positive b, approaching 3*C*M*L/[4*pi*G*b²*(b+L)], and the effective excess mass tends to C*M*L/G. The outer extra force therefore returns to inverse-square form. Only the interval b much smaller than r much smaller than L approaches g=C*M/r and a flat circular-speed contribution; b/L=.05 offers limited separation of these limits.

At b=0 the equivalent density is M_effective_total*L/[4*pi*r²*(r+L)²], the Jaffe profile convention, and its integral is the same total effective mass. This is an interpretation of a static force law, not evidence that extra material exists. The unsoftened center has divergent negative pair potential. Positive b instead bounds each pair below by -(G/b+C*ln(1+L/b))*m_i*m_j. The completed-square terms and kinetic terms are nonnegative. For positive particle masses at fixed total mass, sum over pairs m_i*m_j is at most total_mass²/2, so this supplies a finite bound even under subdivision; it does not establish extensivity when total mass grows with particle count.

Static oscillator elimination requires q=h. Undamped evolution does not generically settle there, and moving sources need not maintain it. The full mechanical force uses the actual q through omega²*q*dh/dr, not its instantaneous minimizing replacement unless that approximation is justified. A measured static profile cannot distinguish the oscillator mechanism from a directly postulated pair potential with the same formula. There is no demonstrated mass-triggered Newtonian suppression, unique relativistic metric, lensing prediction, actual clock measurement, or observational fit here. Changing omega at fixed eta also changes C and hence the static strength; that is not a comparison at fixed static law.
