# More general spherical sources: three feasible, two unresolved

Positive thin spherical shells allow density to rise and fall with radius. Using all measured radii, their geometric midpoints and three exterior grids, projected shell mixtures satisfy every recovered mass bracket for A2142, A85 and ZW1215. A1795 requires at least 5.78% extra allowance in the tested grids; A2319 requires 31.82%. These are fractions of measured mass added outside the existing brackets, not estimated observational errors or statistical significances.

All 15 optimizations were checked against the original constraints. The shell projection also reproduces an independently integrated uniform sphere in six analytic controls. Thin shells are singular feasibility probes: they are not yet differentiable matter distributions suitable for the full gravity action.

## A2319 has a grid-independent geometric conflict

For any nonnegative spherical mass distribution, two projected enclosed masses must satisfy

    Mprojected(R1) >= [1-sqrt(1-(R1/R2)^2)] * Mprojected(R2),  R1<R2.

Every individual shell satisfies this inequality: its minimum ratio occurs at shell radius R2. Nonnegative combinations preserve it. Thus it applies beyond the particular shell grid.

For A2319, R1=8.076 kpc and R2=30.958 kpc give a minimum fraction of 0.034626. The outer lower mass bound is 2.44e11 solar masses, requiring at least about 8.45e9 solar masses projected inside R1. The inner upper bound is only 6.30e9. No positive spherical distribution can satisfy both brackets simultaneously. Under the same common fractional-relaxation definition, this pair alone requires at least 14.46% extra allowance. This lower bound is distinct from the full finite-grid result of 31.82%.

The pair test does not certify A1795's finite-grid infeasibility. It remains a representation or multi-radius consistency problem to investigate. Nor does passing this necessary pair condition prove that a profile has a valid spherical deprojection.

This is a deterministic conflict between source assumptions and simultaneous brackets, not a statistical rejection of the observations or a gravity law. Projected substructure, the adopted center, correlations, confidence coverage and file geometry remain to be assessed. The next step cannot be to drop the conflicting clusters or tune gravity to compensate. It requires a justified source treatment, potentially beyond spherical symmetry.
