# Spherical-source conflicts localize to central projected apertures

Ran a conditional source-only diagnostic on all five stellar profiles at four preset minimum projected radii: 0, 0.02, 0.1 and 0.5 times the inherited R500. This localizes incompatibility; it does not authorize removing measurements from the final source model.

The full-radius shell calculations reproduce the previous results: A1795 requires 5.78% extra bracket allowance and A2319 31.82%; the other three need none. With only radii at or above 0.02R500 constrained, all five have nonnegative shell solutions inside every retained bracket. The same holds for the two larger cutoffs. All 20 linear-program solutions were checked against their constraints.

For A1795 and A2319, 0.02R500 is approximately 23.1 and 27.4 kpc. Their first pressure targets are approximately 861 and 893 kpc, respectively. Thus the demonstrated central source incompatibility and the previously found outer force reversals are distinct issues; one must not be assumed to explain the other.

Possible next source models should account explicitly for central stellar structure and centering, and recover a smooth three-dimensional outer distribution with uncertainties. The current shell solutions are singular and nonunique. Their feasibility neither validates a central model nor provides the density derivatives required by the gravity action. Inner mass still contributes to outer gravity, and the nonlinear source response requires its own justification.

The next physical calculation must retain the unresolved central observations and all clusters. No reduced-aperture source is promoted to a complete reconstruction, no pressure scores were computed, and no gravity candidate was admitted or excluded.

Saved evidence: `stellar-shell-aperture-001`, with all coefficients, retained/omitted source counts, checked bracket violations and the executable diagnostic. The cutoffs use the inherited reference radii only; they were not chosen by optimizing gravity residuals.
