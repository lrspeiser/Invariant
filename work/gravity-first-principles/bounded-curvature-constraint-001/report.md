# Structural constraint on bounded local curvature screening

The curved-background warning is not merely an unfortunate choice of interpolation parameters.

Fix the auxiliary gradient and vary its Hessian along H(t)=H0+t*n*n. For the local action P(x,h), define F(t)=P(x,h(t)). Its second derivative is

    F''(t) = (2 ell²/a0²) B(n),
    B(n) = P_h + 2 ell² P_hh (n.H.n)²/a0².

Requiring B<=0 on the entire ray makes F concave everywhere. A differentiable concave function on the real line that is bounded below must be constant: a nonzero slope gives a tangent upper bound tending to minus infinity in one direction, contradicting the lower bound. Therefore a bounded-below, nonconstant curvature response cannot satisfy that requirement globally.

For the retained action, P is bounded below along these rays and its curvature dependence is nonconstant. Thus global absence of the pressureless short-wavelength growth term cannot be obtained simply by selecting a different shape parameter. This extends the preceding sampled warning under explicit assumptions; it does not say every physical background is unstable.

Two symbolic examples illustrate the same issue. For f(h)=exp(-h), f'+2h*f'' becomes positive above h=1/2. For f(h)=(1+h)^(-p), p>0, it becomes positive above h=1/(2p+1). These are illustrative constitutive functions, not newly fitted gravity laws.

In the actual registered action at x=1 on a rank-one Hessian ray, numerical sign crossings occur near h=0.4892, 0.5273 and 0.5636 for shapes 0.5, 1 and 2. Full curved-background flux checks are retained separately.

## Scope and research consequence

The obstruction assumes a local instantaneous Hessian action, the derived matter coupling, smooth constitutive dependence on the full ray, and the pressureless continuum criterion on every admissible background. Pressure-supported matter, restricted domains justified by microphysics, additional dynamical fields or genuinely nonlocal actions require separate analysis. A mathematical ray does not by itself establish that every point is an admissible isolated equilibrium.

The next theory work should examine a physically specified alternative to these assumptions, with conservation and the full variation derived before fitting observations. An arbitrary cutoff or a retuned bounded local function is not a demonstrated solution. No observational exclusion, covariant ghost proof, or exhaustive statement about gravity laws is claimed.
