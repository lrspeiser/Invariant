# Force weakening and short-wavelength response

The strongest force weakening in the finite positive-source scan occurs for M=1, L=0.1, ell=10, shape=2 at r=1.9953, in units G=a=a0=1. The first-gradient term supplies 1.3949 times the Newtonian force, while the filtered reaction term contributes -0.7980 times it. The net inward force is 0.5969 times Newtonian. The weakening is therefore a property of the complete action response; it cannot be removed by omitting the reaction term without changing the theory.

For frozen background coefficients, the static density-to-gravity transfer has the form

    T(k,n) = A(n) + ell² B(n) k²/(1+L²k²)²
    A = Px + 2 Pxx (grad psi dot n)²
    B = Ph + 2 ell² Phh (n dot Hessian(chi) dot n)².

Here a0=1, chi=S psi and the derivatives are of the same registered action. In the arbitrarily short-wavelength limit T tends to A. Unlike the local predecessor, the filtered curvature term does not grow without bound as k². A direct finite-difference check of the radial first-gradient derivative agrees with the analytic coefficient to 2.47851e-10 relative error (predeclared tolerance 1e-6).

Across all 81 source/parameter combinations, 41 radii and the radial and tangential directions, A ranges from 0.11378576 to 841.92772, with no negative sampled A. This covers both endpoint directions; A is linear in the squared directional cosine on these spherical backgrounds. The large maximum occurs in weak-field regions and is not itself a growth rate: dynamics also depend on source density and the matter model.

The frozen formula permits negative transfer at intermediate wavelengths in 1024 of the 6642 directional samples. These are not 1024 distinct theories or observed failures. Finite-wavelength use of a frozen background is particularly limited when the filter reaches across substantial background variation. A negative static transfer also does not by itself imply instability: with a local cold-fluid closure, sigma²=4pi G rho T, so negative T gives an oscillatory rather than growing density mode. That closure is illustrative, not a derived relativistic or equilibrium matter theory.

The conclusion is narrow: the tested filtered action avoids the specific unbounded local curvature contribution and retains inward background forces in the preceding scan. Neither result establishes nonlinear stability, equilibrium, correct rotation curves, cluster dynamics, lensing, or Solar System compliance. The next meaningful dynamical test needs actual finite positive-source perturbations rather than a frozen-coefficient extrapolation.
