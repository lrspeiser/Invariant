# Where the broader hypotheses stand

This is an evidence map, not a ranking by how appealing the ideas sound. A good numerical solution and a good fit to observed motion are different achievements.

| Idea | Concrete implementation tested | What is useful | What still prevents a physical conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gravity returned or re-emitted over distance | Finite distributed spatial-response kernel; NGC2976 tracer-based conditional 3D sources, multiple quadratures and source depths | Stable numerical predictions; geometry changes force direction and strength | The kernel is a static representation, not a derived reflection/transport process. No independent motion or lensing validation. |
| Low-density coherence/refraction | Variable-permittivity Poisson equation; local and Gaussian-averaged density alternatives | Neighborhood averaging yields more stable field calculations; explicit off-plane predictions | Local-density numerical failure retained. The averaging scale and response law are assumptions. Local-G calibration, actual depth and observed response remain unresolved. |
| Surrounding matter/external gravity | Imposed uniform field in the fixed density-dependent medium, with larger boxes and finer grids | Can separate differential response from uniform acceleration of the whole system | The imposed field is not the observed environment; numerical convergence cannot establish an astronomical external-field effect. |
| Motion or whirlpool-like coupling | Conservative finite-particle kinetic coupling and co-/counter-rotating arrangements | Source-partition bug fixed; momentum and energy checks permit controlled dynamics | Tested weak couplings reduce the initial inward acceleration; stronger couplings disperse configurations. No suitable observed velocity-field fit. |
| Time/memory as an energy reservoir | Explicit internal oscillator coupled to orbital dynamics | Defined energy exchange can conserve a total energy budget | Tested equilibrium adds no static inward force. This does not establish time itself as an energy source or supply relativistic clock/lensing behavior. |
| Radial proxy combinations | Continuous fitting and population-transfer checks on 102 exposed galaxies | Tests many parameter changes against matched baselines | Apparent small gains are fragile and dominated by individual objects. Radial fits do not test full geometry or establish a mechanism. |

The strongest current progress is in making these alternatives calculable and exposing measurement/model assumptions. There is no robust observational winner. Newton/MOND comparisons remain reference tests; excluding a dark-matter term from candidate equations does not establish that dark matter is absent.
