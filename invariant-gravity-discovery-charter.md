# Invariant Gravity Discovery Charter

The project needs to move from **searching for better fitting curves** to **searching for a generative description of a gravitational universe**.

The earlier exhaustive run already showed why: every three-term law expressible by the existing 1,898-atom, point-local grammar was evaluated, and none beat the radial acceleration relation on held-out galaxies. Increasing complexity improved the training fit while worsening the blind result. The standard galaxy variables also collapsed to only two independent numerical directions on that bench, so generating more powers and exponents from the same columns cannot create new physical information.

The next system must therefore search over **complete physical scenes, field structures, paths, directions, histories, and alternate underlying rules**.

## The clear text-based goal

> **Discover the simplest universal law of gravity or spacetime that starts only from observable matter, energy, motion, light, and their complete spatial and temporal arrangement, and predicts the behavior of massive objects and photons across all accessible scales.**
>
> The law must determine whether gravity depends only on local matter or also on the emptiness, geometry, direction, connectivity, motion, and history of the space between concentrations of matter. It must treat individual galaxies, gas structures, stars or stellar populations, and light paths before any averaging is imposed. It must use one global set of physical constants, predict both internal motion within galaxies and motion through groups and clusters, reproduce known high-precision local gravity, and explain or sharply characterize the remaining galaxy–cluster discrepancy.
>
> Invariant must eliminate candidates that depend on arbitrary coordinate choices, an arbitrary zero of potential, catalog deblending, hidden object-specific parameters, non-identifiable quantities, or inconsistent conservation rules. It must test surviving laws against synthetic alternate universes and untouched real observations. Its final output must be either a new generative field law with distinctive successful predictions, or a precise statement of which broad classes of new gravity have been ruled out and which observation would distinguish the remaining equivalence classes.

That should be the controlling objective. Confirming MOND or improving an audit is not the objective. Those are benchmarks and safeguards.

# What the final law must tell us

A satisfactory law must answer all of these questions from the same underlying description.

| Question                                       | Required answer                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What creates gravity?**                      | Which properties of matter, energy, stress, radiation, vacuum, information, or geometry act as sources.                                                    |
| **What is the gravitational state?**           | Whether it is a scalar potential, vector field, tensor response, changing geometry, network state, nonlocal relationship, or combination.                  |
| **What establishes direction?**                | The source shape, angular momentum, external field, tidal axes, neighboring wells, light path, or a new independent field.                                 |
| **Is the response local?**                     | Whether the state at one point depends only on that point, a surrounding volume, the path between sources, the past light cone, or the system's history.   |
| **How does matter move?**                      | Orbits, rotation, vertical motion, velocity dispersion, streams, galaxy motion within clusters, and structure formation.                                   |
| **How does light move?**                       | Deflection, shear, magnification, multiple images, time delays, frequency shifts, polarization, and arrival times.                                         |
| **Do matter and light see the same geometry?** | Whether the equivalence principle holds exactly, approximately, or in an environment-dependent way.                                                        |
| **Why do galaxies and clusters differ?**       | Which measurable variable changes the gravitational response between a disk galaxy, group, cluster outskirts, and cluster core.                            |
| **What happens locally?**                      | Why Solar-System, binary-pulsar, laboratory, and gravitational-wave tests recover established behavior.                                                    |
| **How does the universe form?**                | Whether the law grows structure, forms disks and clusters, produces the cosmic web, and remains stable.                                                    |
| **What creates redshift?**                     | Expansion, clock evolution, path geometry, vacuum response, or another mechanism—and how the same mechanism produces time dilation and distance relations. |
| **What would falsify it?**                     | A specific observation that cannot be repaired by changing object-specific nuisance parameters.                                                            |

A model that answers only "how fast does a star orbit in a disk?" is not a final gravitational law.

# What "all parameters" should mean

No finite list can include every imaginable hidden property of the universe. The scientifically usable target is a complete, extensible ontology containing every quantity that is:

1. directly observed;
2. constructible invariantly from observations;
3. a latent field introduced by an alternate universe with a definite observational consequence;
4. a universal constant or transition scale; or
5. an observational nuisance that must be marginalized rather than mistaken for physics.

Invariant should keep these five classes separate.

| Class                         | Meaning                                                                           | How Invariant uses it                                  |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Direct observable**         | Detector counts, positions, spectra, times, image shapes, velocities              | Primary evidence                                       |
| **Derived physical quantity** | Mass-density posterior, temperature field, stellar population, geometric distance | Input with propagated uncertainty                      |
| **Invariant descriptor**      | Tidal eigenvalues, density contrast, alignment angle, network connectivity        | Candidate explanatory variable                         |
| **Latent physical field**     | Vacuum state, preferred direction, coherence, nonmetricity, memory                | Allowed only through a generative law                  |
| **Nuisance variable**         | PSF, distance error, inclination, calibration, selection                          | Marginalized; not promoted as physics without evidence |

Coefficients and exponents are not independent "things in the universe." They are parameters of candidate laws. Invariant may search billions of coefficient-and-exponent combinations, but the final law should have a small number of universal constants.

| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |

| **Local neighborhood**     | Value smoothed over a declared scale                     |

| **Shell value**            | Value in a radial or geometric layer                     |

| **Path integral**          | Accumulation along a matter or light trajectory          |

| **Difference to boundary** | Gauge-safe comparison with a defined endpoint            |

* internal acceleration together with external tidal orientation;

* a galaxy's surface density together with the age and orientation of the external tidal field.

The system should then use rank analysis, conditional independence, and synthetic identifiability to determine which of these are genuinely new directions and which are algebraic rewritings of existing information.

# The root-data rule

## Invariant owns the averaging

The governing rule should be:

> **Store the finest defensible observation and apply each candidate universe before averaging. Never replace a resolved scene with an averaged source unless the candidate law has been shown to commute with that averaging operation.**

This matters because:

* nonlinear laws do not generally commute with averaging;
* directional laws are erased by azimuthal averaging;
* network laws are erased by replacing galaxies with a smooth profile;
* path laws are erased by radial averaging;
* memory laws are erased by using only a present-day profile;
* strong-lensing information is erased by reducing multiple images to one mass estimate.

The program's earlier A2029-like experiment found that replacing 300 member galaxies with a spherical distribution changed shell-averaged QUMOND gravity by only about \(0.4\%\) and projected deflection at roughly the percent level. That means lumpiness does not explain a factor-of-two cluster discrepancy under QUMOND, but it also means resolved source data become essential once the target precision reaches one or two percent.

## "Movement of a star" in a cluster galaxy

For most distant cluster galaxies, we cannot track the proper motion of a named individual star. The root observable is instead a spatially resolved spectrum. Each spatial element contains a distribution of stellar line-of-sight velocities. Invariant should predict that complete distribution after projection, PSF convolution, and aperture integration.

For nearer systems, discrete planetary nebulae, globular clusters, masers, and resolved stars can serve as individual tracers. Those systems provide the cleanest same-object test of radial versus perpendicular gravity.

SAMI's final release contains integral-field data for 3,068 galaxies, including 888 cluster galaxies, making it useful for internal stellar and gas kinematics in different environments. ([ADS Abs star][1])

## "Movement of light" through a cluster

The direct data are not a lensing mass map. They are:

* the measured shapes of background galaxies;
* the positions and morphologies of multiple images;
* source redshifts;
* magnification information;
* and time delays.

Every candidate universe must propagate photon bundles through its own predicted spacetime and return those observables.

The Hubble Frontier Fields archive contains the imaging for six deep cluster fields; CLASH-VLT obtained more than 31,000 spectra across 12 CLASH clusters, and MUSE observations of Abell 2744 produced a combined catalog of 514 spectroscopic redshifts covering the multiply imaged core. These are pieces of the required root scene, although no single release supplies the complete packet. ([MAST][2])

# Existing data already cover pieces of the scene

No current public corpus perfectly combines every layer, but the components exist:

* SPARC provides 175 late-type galaxies with 3.6-micron photometry and high-quality H I and Hα rotation curves; it remains the baseline radial benchmark rather than the final root-data standard. ([Astroweb][3])
* eFEDS has a weak-lensing-calibrated sample of 313 systems covered by HSC, useful for statistical group-to-cluster transition tests. ([ADS Abs star][4])
* SAMI supplies spatially resolved stellar and gas spectroscopy for a substantial cluster-galaxy sample. ([ADS Abs star][1])
* Hubble Frontier Fields, CLASH-VLT, and MUSE provide deep imaging, strong-lensing constraints, member redshifts, and internal spectroscopy for selected rich clusters. ([MAST][2])
* X-COP publishes thermodynamic, mass, gas-mass, and abundance products for 12 clusters, useful for diffuse baryon modeling and pipeline validation. ([Dominique Eckert][5])

The immediate data-engineering task is therefore to create a **linked corpus**, not to wait for one perfect survey.

* shear catalogs;
* multiple images;
* time delays;
* redshifts;
* and measurement covariance.

Invariant then analyzes the mock universe without being told its generating law.

## Essential benchmark universes

The synthetic suite should always include:

1. standard gravity with baryons only;
2. standard gravity with collisionless dark matter;
3. a MOND/AQUAL-like scalar universe;
4. a scalar environment-dependent universe;
5. a tensor, direction-dependent vacuum universe;
6. a reciprocal nonlocal well-network universe;
7. a universe with gravitational memory;
8. a universe in which photons and matter couple differently;
9. a geometric path-redshift universe;
10. a universe containing only realistic astrophysical and observational systematics.

The purpose is not merely injection recovery. It is to determine which fundamentally different universes are observationally indistinguishable on the current data.

When two universes cannot be separated, Invariant should report:

> These theories belong to the same observational equivalence class on this corpus; the following missing observation would separate them.

That is better than arbitrarily choosing the best-scoring formula.

# How this feeds into the Invariant system

## Stage 0: Define the universe grammar

The grammar should specify:

* allowed source types;
* allowed field ranks;
* allowed notions of locality;
* allowed directions;
* allowed memory and path operations;
* allowed matter–light relationships;
* required conservation and symmetry properties.

The final search is then over sparse combinations of admissible physical building blocks, not arbitrary columns.

## Stage 1: Assemble probabilistic scenes

Convert root observations into posterior ensembles of:

* matter distributions;
* velocities;
* temperatures;
* galaxy memberships;
* line-of-sight depths;
* light-source positions;
* instrument responses.

Do not collapse uncertainty to a best-fit scene.

## Stage 2: Generate the invariant parameter bank

For every scene, calculate the complete parameter ontology at:

* multiple spatial scales;
* multiple directions;
* multiple derivative orders;
* source–source paths;
* source–observer paths;
* current and historical states.

Tag every atom by units, tensor rank, scale, locality, gauge, provenance, and covariance.

## Stage 3: Prune without opening the target data

Reject or classify candidates using:

* dimensional consistency;
* coordinate and frame invariance;
* potential-gauge invariance;
* mass-partition and coarse-graining consistency;
* reciprocity;
* variational or conservation consistency;
* causal validity;
* stable boundary behavior;
* Newtonian or relativistic recovery where required;
* well-posed evolution;
* positive-energy and stability conditions.

The compiler should distinguish:

* mathematically inconsistent;
* physically incomplete;
* convention dependent;
* non-identifiable on the available data;
* admissible.

A non-identifiable theory is not necessarily physically false; it requires a different experiment.

## Stage 4: Remove redundant information

Apply:

* numerical rank;
* conditional independence;
* mutual information;
* sensitivity Jacobians;
* Fisher-information analysis;
* symbolic dependency detection.

This prevents Invariant from treating radius, enclosed mass, surface density, and acceleration as four independent discoveries when they may collapse to two information directions.

## Stage 5: Test identifiability in alternate universes

Before searching real observations, ask:

* Can the system recover an injected scalar law?
* Can it distinguish scalar misspecification from genuine anisotropy?
* Can it recover an external axis?
* Can it distinguish network dependence from source ellipticity?
* Can it detect a path effect after survey systematics?
* Does it falsely detect new gravity in a standard dark-matter universe?
* At what amplitude does each effect become observable?

Use separate simulations for calibration and final audit.

## Stage 6: Search billions of sparse laws

The useful strategy is:

$$
\text{billions of candidate sparse structures}
\quad\text{not}\quad
\text{one law with millions of coefficients}.
$$

A candidate may draw from:

* scalar state variables;
* vector directions;
* tensor bases;
* spatial derivatives;
* nonlocal kernels;
* graph operators;
* path operators;
* memory operators.

But the surviving law should contain only a small number of universal terms and constants.

## Stage 7: Solve each law exactly

Cheap approximations and surrogates may screen candidates, but every survivor must be rerun through:

* the exact field solver;
* the exact source geometry;
* the same boundary conditions;
* the instrument forward model;
* the complete nuisance covariance.

## Stage 8: Demand cross-channel prediction

Fit a candidate in one channel and freeze it before testing another.

Examples:

* fit disk rotation, predict polar motion;
* fit galaxy dynamics, predict weak lensing;
* fit group outskirts, predict cluster cores;
* fit matter motion, predict photon time delays;
* fit present-day fields, predict structure growth.

A model should not be allowed to learn separate gravity laws for each channel.

## Stage 9: Run counterfactual source tests

For every cluster candidate, compare:

* actual galaxy positions;
* same galaxies smoothed into a radial profile;
* same radii with randomized angles;
* same positions with masses permuted;
* alternative line-of-sight depths;
* merged and split catalog representations;
* rotated external tidal axis;
* diffuse-light reassignment within measurement uncertainty;
* foreground and background structure perturbations.

A well-network law must perform better on the actual network than on mass-preserving scrambles.

## Stage 10: Use untouched confirmation systems

Separate:

* training data;
* development/validation data;
* final confirmation data.

Once any numerical result has been examined, that sample is no longer pristine confirmation data.

# The decisive resolved-cluster experiment

The first full experiment should be a **nested cluster scene test**.

## Development set

Choose several clusters with the strongest overlap of:

* deep optical and infrared imaging;
* member spectroscopy;
* internal member-galaxy spectroscopy;
* X-ray and SZ data;
* wide-field weak lensing;
* strong-lensing images and time delays;
* surrounding large-scale-structure data.

Use separate clusters as an untouched confirmation set.

## For each cluster

### 1. Build every visible source separately

Include:

* every member galaxy above the completeness threshold;
* a probabilistic unresolved population;
* the brightest central galaxy;
* intracluster light;
* resolved gas;
* compact sources;
* line-of-sight structures.

### 2. Sample allowed three-dimensional scenes

Do not pretend projected galaxy positions determine exact depth. Generate an ensemble consistent with:

* redshifts;
* cluster phase space;
* morphology;
* substructure;
* spatial selection.

### 3. Solve each alternate universe

Each candidate returns one common gravitational or spacetime state for the scene.

### 4. Predict four nested channels

| Scale                      | Prediction                                                                                 |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Inside member galaxies** | Stellar and gas velocity maps, dispersions, vertical or asymmetric response to the cluster |
| **Across the cluster**     | Member-galaxy velocity distribution and orbital structure                                  |
| **Diffuse plasma**         | Density, temperature, pressure, shocks, and equilibrium or disequilibrium                  |
| **Through the cluster**    | Weak shear, multiple images, arc shapes, magnification, and time delays                    |

This is the central requirement: **the same field that moves a star inside one member galaxy must bend a background light ray passing through the cluster**.

### 5. Score raw observables

Do not score:

* a hydrostatic mass;
* an NFW lensing mass;
* a precomputed convergence map;
* or a single velocity-dispersion estimate

as though it were the primitive observation.

Score detector-facing quantities after the candidate law and the instrument model have both been applied.

### 6. Freeze and transfer

A successful law must transfer:

* from development clusters to an untouched cluster;
* from cluster interiors to member-galaxy internal dynamics;
* from matter tracers to photons;
* and from one survey pipeline to another.

# The minimum data program

## Corpus A: local and high-precision gravity

Purpose: stop new laws from hiding violations in the high-acceleration regime.

* Solar-System ranging and Doppler
* Out-of-ecliptic spacecraft
* Wide binaries
* Pulsar timing
* Gravitational-wave timing
* Laboratory equivalence tests

## Corpus B: resolved isolated galaxies

Purpose: retain the galaxy regularities any new law must inherit.

* Raw H I and molecular-gas cubes
* Optical and infrared photometry
* Stellar-population information
* Two-dimensional velocity fields
* Vertical stellar dynamics
* Warped and polar tracers
* Streams and satellites

SPARC remains a useful benchmark, but the final analysis should increasingly use resolved images and data cubes rather than only published one-dimensional rotation curves. ([Astroweb][3])

## Corpus C: field-versus-cluster galaxies

Purpose: test whether the same galaxy behaves differently when embedded in a deeper or more directional environment.

* Matched stellar mass, size, morphology, and surface density
* Spatially resolved stellar kinematics
* Gas kinematics as a contamination and environmental diagnostic
* Clustercentric position
* External field and tidal axes
* Stripping and interaction flags

## Corpus D: statistical groups and clusters

Purpose: map the continuous transition rather than classify objects as "galaxy" or "cluster."

* Hundreds of systems
* Raw shear or source-shape catalogs
* Resolved baryon profiles
* Wide mass and radius coverage
* Uniform selection
* Shared covariance

eFEDS–HSC is a natural part of this bridge because the sample reaches hundreds of systems with weak-lensing coverage. ([ADS Abs star][4])

## Corpus E: gold resolved clusters

Purpose: test geometry, direction, network effects, and matter–light consistency.

* Hubble-quality imaging
* MUSE or comparable spectroscopy
* wide-field weak lensing
* X-ray and SZ
* member galaxy IFU data
* strong-lensing image families
* time delays
* surrounding structure

## Corpus F: redshift and cosmology

Purpose: test the redshift branch independently of the gravity branch.

* Raw supernova light curves and spectra
* Geometric distance anchors
* Standard sirens
* BAO observables
* CMB spectrum, anisotropy, and lensing
* Void and filament maps
* Host and line-of-sight data

A candidate redshift mechanism must predict both frequency shift and event-duration stretching.

# What counts as discovering new gravity

A final candidate should not be promoted unless it satisfies all of the following.

1. **It introduces a genuine physical dependence absent from the RAR**, such as direction, path, network, memory, or a new spacetime state.

2. **It is generative.** Given a complete source scene and boundary conditions, it produces the gravitational or spacetime state rather than merely returning a fitted acceleration ratio.

3. **It uses one global parameter set.** Object-specific distances and mass uncertainties are allowed; object-specific gravity constants are not.

4. **It predicts multiple probes.** The same state must move matter, bend light, and—if claimed—alter clocks and redshift.

5. **It preserves known limits.** Solar-System, binary, wave-propagation, and high-acceleration behavior must be recovered.

6. **It matches galaxy regularities.** It cannot explain clusters by destroying the RAR, flat rotation curves, or baryonic Tully–Fisher relation.

7. **It improves the cluster problem using root observations.** It must predict raw shear, strong-lens images, gas, and member dynamics—not only a derived mass.

8. **It survives representation changes.** Splitting a galaxy into catalog subcomponents or changing grid resolution must not change the physical result.

9. **It survives alternate-universe controls.** The search must recover the right family when injected and avoid finding it in scalar, dark-matter, or systematics-only worlds.

10. **It makes a distinctive sealed prediction.** At least one observation must be predicted before its result is examined.

11. **It is sparse enough to explain.** Compute may explore billions of candidates, but the result should be comprehensible as a small set of physical relationships.

12. **It can evolve.** A final theory must be stable and capable of generating cosmic structure, not only fitting a frozen snapshot.

# Copy-ready directive for Invariant

> **Invariant shall search for a universal generative law of gravity or spacetime using root observations rather than pre-averaged or theory-derived mass estimates. Each observed system shall be represented as a probabilistic four-dimensional scene containing all resolved matter components, their motion and state, surrounding environment, individual tracer populations, and photon paths. In clusters, every member galaxy shall remain an individual source down to the completeness threshold, with unresolved sources represented statistically; diffuse gas, the central galaxy, intracluster light, and line-of-sight structure shall remain separate. The same candidate law must predict internal stellar and gas motion within member galaxies, galaxy motion through the cluster, diffuse gas behavior, weak lensing, strong lensing, and time delays.**
>
> **Invariant shall generate scalar, vector, tensor, graph, path, and history descriptors from the complete parameter ontology at multiple scales. Each descriptor shall carry units, transformation properties, scale, locality, gauge convention, uncertainty, covariance, causal status, and coarse-graining behavior. Candidate laws shall be pruned before fitting for dimensional inconsistency, coordinate or potential-gauge dependence, catalog-partition dependence, nonreciprocity, lack of a conservation mechanism, unstable boundaries, failure of known local limits, redundancy, and non-identifiability.**
>
> **The discovery system shall explore alternate universes that differ in source type, field type, locality, directionality, memory, propagation, geometry, effective dimensionality, matter–light coupling, vacuum behavior, and cosmological evolution. Each alternate universe shall generate synthetic detector-level observations with the same noise and selection as the real surveys. Invariant shall demonstrate calibrated recovery and false-positive control on those universes before interpreting a real-data result.**
>
> **The search may evaluate billions of sparse candidate structures, but every surviving law must use a small universal parameter set and must transfer across systems and observational channels with its parameters frozen. The final result shall be either a new action-consistent generative law with a successful untouched prediction, or an explicit equivalence-class map identifying which broad theories remain observationally indistinguishable and the exact missing observation required to separate them.**

The most important conceptual shift is:

$$
\boxed{\text{Do not ask which formula best fits a table. Ask which universe best generates the entire observation.}}
$$

That gives Invariant a real path toward a new form of gravity: the search space becomes the possible **states and behaviors of spacetime**, while the data remain the complete motions of matter and light rather than summaries produced under an assumed theory.
