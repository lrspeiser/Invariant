Abstract

Two earlier gravity receipts screened a declared grammar of modified-gravity laws against frozen synthetic geometries and left 23 candidates in 12 equivalence families standing. Both said in their own claims block that no observational data had been opened. This one opens it. Each surviving family’s law, its covariant action, and the rotation speed that follows from that action were written out in closed form, and the prediction was built from published photometry and gas columns alone — no dark halo, no unseen component, and no quantity anywhere that is free to differ from one galaxy to the next. Against 214 published measurements from 6 galaxies, 0 of the 12 families reach every published error bar with one universal parameter set. The published baryons alone do not reach them either, which is the control that had to fire and did.

Data: Lelli F., McGaugh S. S., Schombert J. M., 2016, AJ 152, 157 (2016AJ....152..157L), 'SPARC: Mass Models for 175 Disk Galaxies with Spitzer Photometry and Accurate Rotation Curves' (doi:10.26093/cds/vizier.51520157).

The question

A rotation curve is the plainest evidence for the missing-mass problem: the gas and stars you can see do not have enough gravity to hold the outer disk at the speed it is measured to move. A modified-gravity candidate earns the right to be taken seriously only if it closes that gap without inventing the mass, and without a knob that can be turned differently for each galaxy. So the question asked here is narrow and answerable:

Is the predicted rotation speed reachable inside the published uncertainties, when the only free quantities are universal constants shared by every galaxy at once?

a declared set of six long-studied galaxies chosen before any fit was run, to span the widest available baryonic mass range in the catalogue: one gas-rich dwarf, three intermediate disks, one bulge-dominated spiral, one giant spiral V_obs from 13.80 km/s (DDO154 innermost) to 305.00 km/s (UGC02885 innermost); flat velocities span roughly 47 to 298 km/s, a factor above 6 in velocity and above 1000 in baryonic mass

The stellar mass-to-light ratios are the usual place a per-galaxy knob hides. Here they are fixed at their published values — 1/2 for the disk and 7/10 for the bulge, in M_Sun/L_Sun at 3.6 micron — identical for every galaxy and never fitted.

What we did

The rule that had to change, and the rule that did not

Every surviving candidate is a scalar field coupled to matter, and the field contract as written forbade every direct scalar-matter coupling. That single prohibition was doing two different jobs, so it was split in two and only one half was relaxed.

Version 1.0 forbade every direct scalar-matter coupling, which is the clause that blocks each surviving screened-gravity lift (recorded as residual blocker 'missing_adapter:direct_scalar_matter_coupling' on all twelve STABLE_PASS families in runs/gpu-baryonic-screen/nonlocal-localization-v1.json). That single prohibition conflates two different things. Hidden mass is unfalsifiable bookkeeping: a per-object free mass parameter or an unseen component can absorb any residual, so it is forbidden absolutely and remains so. A declared scalar field carrying a declared coupling that is universal -- one and the same function for every matter species and every object, with no per-object freedom -- is not hidden mass. It is a modification of gravity, it preserves the weak equivalence principle because every species still falls along geodesics of one common metric, and it is falsifiable: the same coupling that fits a galaxy also fixes the Solar-System and cluster predictions, so it can be killed by measurement. Version 1.1 therefore distinguishes the two and permits only the second, under declaration.

The half that did not move is the one that matters: No invisible or unseen mass component, and no per-object free mass parameter, may appear anywhere in the action, the solution, or the fit. A candidate that needs one is rejected, never rescued.

What this does not show

This was exploratory. The universal constants were scanned over a declared grid against the same galaxies the verdict is read from, so fitting and testing used the same data. That is declared, not hidden, and it is why no result here may be cited as a confirmation.

Reusing the data can only make a theory look better, never worse. A FEASIBLE verdict obtained this way would be provisional. An INFEASIBLE verdict obtained this way is the opposite: the family was handed the full declared grid and still could not reach the published intervals, so the exploratory framing strengthens rather than weakens the negative.

A sealed no-refit trial would fix the universal constants first and then open galaxies withheld from this scan. It is reserved and out of scope here.

A verdict here is a statement about these declared intervals, this declared grid, and these six galaxies. It is not a statement about modified gravity in general, it is not a statement about the wider catalogue, and it does not touch the many formal blockers these families still carry from the receipt that produced them. The declared assumptions the result rests on:

  • code unit map. The screen's kernel lengths L1 and L2 and its screening length Lc are pure code numbers sharing one code unit system across all systems. Confronting metres therefore needs exactly two universal constants -- one length unit and one acceleration scale -- both scanned on declared finite ladders and both shared by every galaxy. Declared assumption, not a derivation.
  • gas sign convention. V_gas is published signed, negative where a central HI hole makes the gas contribution point outward. The signed square V_gas|V_gas| preserves that published convention; no absolute value is taken.
  • mass truncated at the last measured radius. No baryonic mass is assumed beyond the outermost published radius. That under-counts the nonlocal source and can only make a family look worse, never better, so it is the conservative direction. Declared assumption, not a derivation.
  • published sigma is a floor. The published e_Vobs is the random error from non-circular motions and kinematic asymmetries and explicitly excludes systematic inclination uncertainty, so the declared interval is narrower than the total observational uncertainty. Verdicts are therefore about the published random interval and are stated as such.
  • screening is phenomenological. S = [1 + (Lc |grad g_N|/g_N)^k]^(-1) is the screen's declared stand-in for K-mouflage gradient screening, carried over verbatim from the generating receipt. The derivation chain below derives the K-mouflage suppression from the action and shows that it has this shape; it does not prove this exact algebraic form.
  • spherical reduction. The published columns give each baryonic component's Newtonian circular-speed contribution. The nonlocal kernel needs a mass distribution, so the enclosed spherically-equivalent baryonic mass M(r) = r V_bar^2(r)/G is reconstructed from those columns and the mass between two published radii is spread uniformly in r. This is a declared reduction of a disk to a spherical profile, not a derivation, and it is the only geometric liberty taken.

Methods

Technical detail — open to expand

Instrument. Each published measurement becomes an exact rational interval and the question asked is feasibility: does one parameter vector pass through every interval at once? For a reachable answer the witness is re-checked against every pooled row in exact rational arithmetic. For an unreachable one the Farkas certificate is re-verified before it is recorded. No residual is minimised, nothing is ranked by how well it fits, and no fit statistic is computed anywhere.

No per-object freedom, structurally. Every galaxy’s rows enter one pooled linear system whose width is the number of universal parameters. The width does not grow when galaxies are added, which is asserted in the tests by building the same system over one, two, and six galaxies. A per-galaxy parameter would have to widen it, and cannot. The baryonic term’s coefficient is pinned to exactly one by a structural constraint carried inside the instrument, so it appears by name in any certificate that uses it.

Uncertainties. Sigma comes from the published error column and is propagated outward into velocity-squared in closed form. The instrument re-derives sigma from the published decimal string wherever its rule permits and refuses any value the rule does not, so widening a tolerance until the answer changes is not expressible.

Universal constants. The kernel’s lengths are pure code numbers, so exactly two universal constants convert them to physical units: an acceleration scale and a length unit. Both are scanned on declared finite ladders — 1e2, 3e2, 1e3, 2e3, 3e3, 5e3, 7e3, 1e4, 2e4, 3e4 in (km/s)²/kpc and 1e-1, 3e-1, 1, 3, 1e1 in kpc — and both are shared by every galaxy.

The nonlocal term. The kernel is a convolution over the baryonic mass, so the published columns are reduced to a spherically equivalent mass profile and the shell average is integrated in closed form in the radial variable. Its endpoint logarithm is handled by a graded mesh; refining the quadrature moves the result by at most 6.13598964e-05 relative, against a declared tolerance of 1e-3. Design columns computed in double precision are cross-checked against a 50-digit recomputation, agreeing to 2.66137700e-16.
