Method: how the machine keeps itself honest

Every claim on this site rode down the same assembly line, and every station on that line exists because some earlier version of this project measured itself doing something embarrassing without it. First the line, then the embarrassments.

The assembly line

  1. Declared families, numbered exhaustively. Every candidate family is enumerable and finite; a survivor is an address in a declared list, never a free-form artifact that wandered in.
  2. GPU screen. Cheap, physics- or math-informative tests over the whole family — single precision first, double precision to confirm — with every threshold recorded in the receipt’s config block.
  3. Exact confirmation. Shortlisted survivors are replayed in high-precision or exact integer arithmetic, and screen-versus-exact crosschecks are receipted with their disagreement counts.
  4. Sealed receipts. Results are canonical JSON, hash-bound (content_sha256), float-free, and carry their own scope and claims fields. Negative verdicts are sealed with the same ceremony.
  5. Formal kernel. What admits a proof is proved in Lean — a proof kernel, meaning a small trusted program that checks every logical step. Exact checking and kernel verification are never merged, and every receipt says which one it has.
  6. Sealed observational ladder. Real data opens once, under a committed protocol, with no refit. To date zero real observations have been opened — documented in repository docs (GOALS_AND_MEASURED_OUTCOMES.md).

The 0/21 versus 21/21 contrast is the founding measurement. The same engine, on the same hidden worlds, failed every blind guess — and then succeeded exactly when public constraints made the answer unique. The honest reading is recorded in the trial document: that success belongs to the solver plus the constraints, and calling it discovery would be recall wearing a costume. Independently, the 70/70 epoch showed a funnel that let candidates die at the expensive formal tier without ever being asked a cheap physics question. Both failures stay on the books, and both dictated the architecture: cheap falsification first, exact confirmation second, formal proof third, sealed data last — with every claim carrying its boundary.

The claim discipline

Binding on every task, quoted from the roadmap’s list:

  • Corpus absence is never novelty.
  • Survival is never proof.
  • A restricted domain is never a global claim.
  • Kernel verification happens only in the kernel.
  • No invisible mass as target or rescue.
  • Sealed data opens once, no refit.
  • Negative receipts are deliverables.

Documented in repository docs (CONTINUOUS_DISCOVERY_ROADMAP.md).
